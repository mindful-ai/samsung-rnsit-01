Section 4. Term, Milestone-Based Activation, and Renewal Options.

4.1 Initial Term and Condition Precedent. This Agreement shall become legally binding on both Parties as of the Effective Date written above. However, the operational duration of this Agreement (the "Initial Term") shall not commence until the satisfaction of the condition precedent set forth herein. Specifically, the Initial Term shall begin on the first calendar day of the month immediately following the Date of Project Acceptance (the "Commencement Date"), which shall be formalized via a mutually executed Certificate of Delivery. The Initial Term shall continue for a fixed period of five (5) consecutive years from the Commencement Date, unless terminated earlier in strict accordance with the provisions of Section 9 (Termination for Cause) or Section 10 (Termination for Convenience) of this Agreement. If Project Acceptance is not achieved within one hundred and eighty (180) calendar days from the Effective Date due to a material breach or delay caused solely by the Vendor, the Customer reserves the unilateral right to dissolve this Agreement without penalty or further liability.

4.2 Automatic Successive Renewal Periods. Upon the expiration of the Initial Term, and subject to the pricing adjustments detailed in Section 4.3, this Agreement shall automatically extend for up to three (3) successive, consecutive periods of twelve (12) months each (each a "Renewal Term"). The Initial Term and any properly activated Renewal Terms are collectively referred to as the "Term." An automatic renewal shall occur seamlessly unless either Party provides formal, written notice of its non-renewal intent to the other Party. To be legally effective, such non-renewal notice must be delivered via registered mail or an approved enterprise electronic signature platform no later than ninety (90) calendar days prior to the expiration of the then-current Initial Term or Renewal Term. Failure to provide timely notice shall bind both Parties to the obligations of the upcoming Renewal Term.

4.3 Rate Adjustments During Renewal Terms. The financial compensation due to the Vendor during any Renewal Term shall not remain static. It shall be adjusted to reflect economic fluctuations, capped at a maximum increase of four percent (4%) over the rates applicable in the immediately preceding year. Alternatively, the increase may be calculated based on the trailing twelve-month percentage change in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), whichever percentage is lesser at the time of renewal. The Vendor must submit the new calculated pricing schedule to the Customer at least one hundred and twenty (120) days before the current term expires. If the Vendor fails to submit this pricing schedule on time, the pricing for the upcoming Renewal Term will default to the rates used in the preceding period.

4.4 Extension for Orderly Transition. In the event that this Agreement expires or is terminated by either Party for any reason other than an uncured material breach by the Customer, the Customer shall have the right to request a temporary extension of services (the "Transition Period"). This Transition Period shall not exceed one hundred and eighty (180) calendar days from the scheduled date of expiration or termination. The explicit purpose of this Transition Period is to facilitate an orderly, non-disruptive migration of data, configurations, and operations to a successor vendor or to an internal system managed directly by the Customer. During the Transition Period, the Vendor is legally obligated to continue providing all services, licenses, and support structures described in this Agreement. The Customer shall compensate the Vendor for these transitional services at the exact prorated rates in effect immediately prior to expiration, without any added penalties, premiums, or termination-related surcharges.

4.5 Survival of Specific Covenants. The expiration, non-renewal, or termination of this Agreement shall not release either Party from obligations that by their nature should survive. This includes, but is not limited to, confidentiality (Section 6), intellectual property indemnification (Section 7), limitation of liability (Section 12), governance laws (Section 15), and the data deletion protocols required upon final separation.
